## Step zero — feasibility assessment (written BEFORE any implementation)

### The question

`Reconciliation/CLAUDE.md` describes the planned `rollback` row as "rewind to authoritative
state, re-apply buffered inputs (GGPO-style)". GGPO-style rollback has three irreducible
ingredients:

1. a **history of inputs** `u_k`,
2. a **snapshot/anchor state** to rewind to, and
3. a **simulation function `f`** to re-apply those inputs through: `s_{k+1} = f(s_k, u_k)`.

The value of rollback over any blending scheme comes entirely from ingredient 3 being
**nonlinear and state-dependent**. If `f` is an affine integrator, rewinding and re-simulating
produces exactly the mispredicted trajectory plus a constant offset, and the whole apparatus is
an expensive way to compute an offset. This is the load-bearing fact for everything below.

### What the contract actually supplies

`Contracts/IReconciler.cs`, and `Pipeline/OperatorEndpoint.ObserveRobotState` /
`EstimateRobotState`, give a reconciler exactly this:

- `Observe(Stamped<Pose> authoritative, Pose predictedAtCapture, in PredictorDiagnostics)` —
  truth at a capture instant, plus the pose that was shown for that same instant.
- `Reconcile(Pose predicted, long nowTicks)` — the predictor's estimate for this frame.

And the registry factory signature fixes the constructor dependencies:

```csharp
Func<ReconcilerConfig, IMetricSink, ITimeAuthority, IReconciler<Pose>> Reconcilers
```

So of the three ingredients the reconciler has **ingredient 2 only** (the authoritative pose is
the anchor). It has:

- **no `u_k`.** The operator command stream (`CommandFrame`: pose, linear/angular velocity,
  gripper) is created in `OperatorEndpoint.SubmitCommand` and handed to the uplink transport. It
  is never handed to the reconciler and there is no parameter on `IReconciler` that could carry
  it. `PredictorDiagnostics` carries horizon/observation counts/sigmas — no inputs.
- **no `f`.** `IRobotPlant` is constructed in `SweepCommand.RunTrial` and injected into
  `RobotEndpoint`, i.e. the *robot* side of the loopback. `OperatorEndpoint` never sees a plant,
  and neither the `IReconciler` interface nor the registry factory has a slot for one.

### Reading A: replay buffered *predictor outputs* / pose deltas. Does it work?

This is the only reading that lives entirely inside `IReconciler<Pose>`: buffer the per-frame
poses the reconciler has already seen, treat their frame-to-frame increments as the "inputs",
rewind to `authoritative`, and re-apply the increments forward to now. `RollbackHistoryCapacity`
would size that buffer.

**It telescopes.** Let `p_k` be the buffered pose sequence (predictor outputs, or equally the
reconciler's own displayed outputs), with `c` the frame at/nearest the authoritative capture
stamp and `n` the current frame. The only re-simulation available without a plant is composition
of the increments:

```
position:     s_n = truth + Σ_{k=c}^{n-1} (p_{k+1} − p_k)
                  = truth + (p_n − p_c)
                  = p_n + (truth − p_c)

orientation:  q_n = q_truth · Π_{k=c}^{n-1} (q_k^{-1} q_{k+1})
                  = q_truth · (q_c^{-1} q_n)
```

Both sums/products telescope — the rotation one too, because the relative rotations are taken
consistently in one frame, so every interior term cancels. The result is
**`currentDisplay ∘ (authoritative ⊖ displayAtCapture)`**, i.e. the current pose with the full
correction error applied in one step.

Three consequences, each individually fatal:

1. **`RollbackHistoryCapacity` provably cannot affect the output.** The re-simulated result is
   independent of how many intermediate frames were buffered — every interior term cancels. A
   history of 4 and a history of 400 produce bit-identical output. When the one config field
   that exists specifically for an algorithm is provably inert in it, the algorithm is not
   actually present. (Corroborating: `SweepCommand.RunTrial` hardcodes
   `rollbackHistoryCapacity: 16` and `ExperimentConfig` does not expose it, so nothing could
   sweep it either.)
2. **It is `snap`.** `currentDisplay + (truth − displayAtCapture)` applied on the correction
   frame is a full-magnitude position step. `SnapReconciler` is already the registry entry for
   "spend the entire correction in one frame". A second registry key for the same behaviour is a
   duplicate, not a candidate — and it would pollute a head-to-head sweep with what looks like an
   independent data point but is the baseline wearing a different name.
3. **It violates requirement 2 (C1 continuity).** A rewind is a position *and* velocity
   discontinuity. `Reconciliation/CLAUDE.md` grants exactly one deliberate exception — `snap`,
   which exists to quantify that cost — and states "every other reconciler still owes the full
   requirement".

#### Telescoping verified numerically, not just algebraically

I did not want the central claim of this assessment resting on algebra I wrote myself, so I
checked it numerically in a scratch project outside the repo (`/tmp/telescope`, not committed,
no repo file touched). The trajectory is deliberately hostile to telescoping: curved, jittered,
non-uniform position steps and a per-frame-varying rotation axis and angle, so a genuine
dependence on the buffered interior frames would show up. Truth is `pos[c]` plus a fixed large
offset (0.9, −0.4, 1.1 m and 0.6 rad), comparable to the ~1.3 m opening transient sweeps produce.

```
depth |  |rollback-snap| (mm)  |  angle(rollback,snap) (deg)  |  |rollback - rollback@depth1| (mm)
------+-----------------------+-----------------------------+----------------------------------
    1 |            0.000E+000 |                  0.000E+000 |                       0.000E+000
    2 |            1.686E-004 |                  0.000E+000 |                       2.384E-004
    4 |            2.666E-004 |                  0.000E+000 |                       2.384E-004
    8 |            2.920E-004 |                  0.000E+000 |                       4.768E-004
   16 |            5.331E-004 |                  0.000E+000 |                       5.331E-004
   64 |            7.633E-004 |                  0.000E+000 |                       7.153E-004
  256 |            1.079E-003 |                  0.000E+000 |                       1.168E-003
  512 |            2.560E-003 |                  0.000E+000 |                       2.590E-003
```

Rollback and snap agree to **2.6e-3 mm at a history depth of 512** — 2.6 nanometres, about
2000x below `ConvergencePositionToleranceMeters` (5 mm) as the sweep configures it. Orientation
agrees **exactly** (0.000 deg at every depth). The residual grows like sqrt(depth), the signature
of float32 accumulation over more addition steps, not of an algorithmic difference; the
depth-independence column is the same magnitude as the rollback-vs-snap column, which is what
you would expect if both are pure rounding.

This is the sanity check my brief asks for in the other direction: I was looking for a
difference that would justify building the thing, and the measurement says there is none.

### Can the discontinuity be hidden? Yes — and that is the collapse

The brief calls C1 "the crux", correctly. The only mechanism available inside `IReconciler<Pose>`
for hiding a rewind is the one `SpringReconciler` already documents: seed a residual offset to
`lastDisplayed − predicted` so the visible pose is continuous across the onset frame, then decay
that offset to zero. Do that to a rollback and the "rollback" part becomes unobservable: the
telescoped re-simulation contributes exactly the offset seed, and everything visible afterwards
is the decay law. What is left is **"re-seed an offset and decay it"** — verbatim the phrasing
in my brief, and verbatim what `spring` does, differing at most in the decay law, which is what
`exp-smooth` and `budget-blend` are for. The history buffer, the rewind and the replay all cancel
out of the visible output.

### The deeper reason: the predictor already performs the rollback

This is not a coincidence of the algebra. `ConstantVelocityPredictor.Predict` is:

```csharp
Stamped<Pose> newest = _history[_count - 1];        // rewind to the newest authoritative sample
...
Vector3 position = newest.Value.Position + velocity * dt;   // re-simulate forward to the target
```

That *is* rewind-to-authoritative-and-re-integrate, executed every frame. `OperatorEndpoint`
calls `_robotStatePredictor.Observe(sample)` before the reconciler ever runs, so by the time
`Reconcile` is called, the predictor's output **already incorporates truth** — the rollback has
happened one component upstream. `SpringReconciler`'s type doc states this outright: "once
`Observe` reaches it, its output already incorporates truth, so displaying it directly *is* the
snap. What this class hides is the jump the predictor's own correction caused."

So on this architecture the reconciler is, by construction, the component that **hides** a
rollback the predictor has already performed. Asking it to perform a second rollback of its own
is asking it to redo work that is upstream of it, from strictly less information (poses only, no
inputs, no plant). It cannot come out ahead, and the algebra above shows it comes out exactly
even.

### Reading B: rollback on late/out-of-order samples

The one genuinely novel use I could find for a history buffer: `spring` and `snap` both drop an
`Observe` whose `CaptureTicks <= _lastAcceptedCaptureTicks`. A rollback reconciler could instead
rewind its *internal offset state* to the frame matching that older stamp and re-integrate the
offset dynamics forward with the late information folded in.

This also collapses, for the same reason:

- Frames already displayed cannot be un-displayed, so the rewind can only change the offset
  state *at now*. The difference between the re-integrated offset and the live one appears as a
  jump in the current visible output — the same C1 violation, now on the frames a lossy link
  produces most often.
- To hide it you must re-seed continuity (`offset := lastDisplayed − predicted`), which
  **overwrites** the re-integrated offset value. All the rewind can leave behind is the offset
  *velocity* — and `SpringReconciler.SeedOffsetFromDisplayedPose` already deliberately carries
  offset velocity across a re-seed for exactly this C1 reason. The residual difference is
  second-order in the correction and would not be separable from noise in a sweep.

### Reading C: give the reconciler its own internal motion model as `f`

Rejected on architecture grounds, not just on measurement grounds. A private integrator inside
the reconciler would be a **second, unaudited plant** in Core, duplicating `Plant/RigidBodyPlant`
while `IRobotPlant`'s own doc says it is "one interface over three very different things" and
"an algorithm that only works against one of them is not a result". Worse, it would be the
*wrong* plant: GGPO is correct precisely because the re-simulation is bit-identical to the
original simulation. Re-simulating against a model that is not the plant that produced the
authoritative sample yields a re-simulation that is wrong in a way proportional to the model
mismatch — strictly worse than not re-simulating. And if the private model is made affine to
keep it tractable, Reading A's telescoping applies again.

### Verdict

**Infeasible within the existing contract, and duplicative of `snap`/`spring` in every reading
that does fit inside it.** Not implemented; no `RollbackReconciler.cs`, no registry line, no
tests. This is the "feasible but duplicative / infeasible without an architecture change"
outcome, and it is a real result: it closes the row rather than leaving it to be re-attempted.

`ReconcilerConfig.RollbackHistoryCapacity` is evidence that somebody intended this to be
buildable, and my brief was right to say weigh it as evidence and not proof. The config field
predates the pipeline wiring. It documents an intention that the `IReconciler` boundary, as
drawn, does not support — and the telescoping argument shows the field could not have had an
observable effect even if a `RollbackReconciler` had been written against that boundary.

### What would have to change, precisely

Genuine rollback needs both missing ingredients delivered to the reconciler:

1. **Ingredient 1 — the input history.** Either `IReconciler` gains an input-observation method
   (`ObserveCommand(in CommandFrame)`, called from `OperatorEndpoint.SubmitCommand`), or a new
   `Contracts/` interface owns the input ring and the reconciler reads it. `RollbackHistoryCapacity`
   would finally size something real. Note this only makes sense operator-side, where the commands
   originate.
2. **Ingredient 2 — the simulation function.** The reconciler needs an `IRobotPlant` it can drive
   to re-simulate. `OperatorEndpoint` currently has none; `RobotEndpoint` owns the only one in the
   loop. Supplying it means either an operator-side *shadow* plant (a second `IRobotPlant`
   instance the operator runs locally as its model of the robot — which is really a client-side
   prediction architecture, not a reconciler) or plumbing the real plant across the endpoint
   boundary, which the loopback structure does not permit and real hardware certainly does not.
3. **The registry factory signature.** `Func<ReconcilerConfig, IMetricSink, ITimeAuthority, IReconciler<Pose>>`
   would have to widen for every existing entry, and `SweepCommand`, `MoveArmCommand` and
   `ClockSyncCheckCommand` would all have to supply the new dependency.
4. Plus the C1 question would still be unanswered: a correct re-simulation still lands on a
   different pose than the one currently displayed, so the rewind still needs hiding, and the
   hiding mechanism is still offset-and-decay. **Rollback would have to be composed with a
   smoother, not compared against one.** That is arguably the most interesting thing in this
   assessment: rollback is not a member of the reconciler axis at all; it is a *predictor-side*
   or *pipeline-side* architecture that a reconciler would then smooth the output of.

Corroborating the "wrong axis" reading: **no contract in `Contracts/` carries an input except
`IRobotPlant.Command(in CommandFrame)`** (and `ICommandCodec`, which only serialises one).
`IPredictor<TState>.Observe(Stamped<TState>)` is state-only too. Inputs exist on the robot side
of the loopback by design. Any rollback design has to cross that boundary or duplicate the plant
on the operator side, and both are architecture decisions.

Per root `CLAUDE.md` — "New question entirely → new interface in `Contracts/`, new folder,
`Pipeline/` learns to wire it. This is an architecture change: write an ADR in `docs/adr/`
first." Items 1–3 are exactly that. **An ADR is a human's decision and I am not writing one, nor
making any of these changes.** Recorded as blocked-on-human.

### Falsifier I applied to my own assessment

I did not want to talk myself out of an implementation, so I fixed in advance what would have
changed my mind: *if I could construct a replay rule, expressible in `IReconciler<Pose>` and
allocation-free, whose output depends observably on `RollbackHistoryCapacity`, then rollback is
real and distinct and I build it.* I could not — the telescoping proof in Reading A shows that no
such rule exists as long as the re-simulation is composition of buffered increments, and any rule
that is *not* composition of the buffered increments is a blend rather than a replay, which is
the `spring`/`budget-blend` family. That is why the verdict is what it is rather than a
preference.
